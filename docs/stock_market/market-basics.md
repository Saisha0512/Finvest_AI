# Stock Market Basics

## What is a Stock Exchange?
A stock exchange, such as the NYSE or NASDAQ, is a regulated marketplace where buyers and sellers trade shares of publicly listed companies. Exchanges provide price transparency, liquidity, and a standardized way for companies to raise capital by selling ownership stakes to the public.

## Market Indices
A market index, such as the S&P 500, Dow Jones Industrial Average, or Nasdaq Composite, tracks the performance of a specific basket of stocks and is used as a benchmark for how "the market" or a segment of it is performing. The S&P 500 tracks 500 of the largest publicly traded U.S. companies and is the most commonly cited broad market benchmark.

## Market Capitalization
Market capitalization is the total value of a company's outstanding shares, calculated as share price multiplied by the number of shares outstanding. Companies are generally grouped as large-cap (over $10 billion), mid-cap ($2-10 billion), and small-cap (under $2 billion). Larger companies tend to be more stable, while smaller companies can offer higher growth potential with higher risk.

## Bull and Bear Markets
A bull market refers to a sustained period of rising prices, generally defined as a 20% or greater rise from a recent low. A bear market refers to a sustained period of falling prices, generally defined as a 20% or greater decline from a recent high. Both are a normal part of market cycles.

## Market Orders vs Limit Orders
A market order executes immediately at the best currently available price. A limit order only executes at a specified price or better, giving the investor price control at the cost of a guaranteed execution. Limit orders are useful for controlling entry and exit prices, especially in volatile or thinly traded stocks.

## Dividends
Some companies distribute a portion of their profits to shareholders as dividends, usually paid quarterly. Dividend-paying stocks are often favored by income-focused investors, though not all companies pay dividends — many growth-oriented companies reinvest profits back into the business instead.
