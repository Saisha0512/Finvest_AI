# Diversification

## What is Diversification?
Diversification means spreading investments across different asset classes, sectors, geographies, and individual securities so that a decline in any single investment has a limited impact on the overall portfolio. The principle is often summarized as "don't put all your eggs in one basket."

## Diversifying Across Asset Classes
Holding a mix of stocks, bonds, cash, and real assets reduces overall portfolio volatility because these asset classes do not move in perfect sync. When equities decline sharply, high-quality bonds often hold steady or even rise, cushioning the overall portfolio.

## Diversifying Within an Asset Class
Within equities alone, diversification means holding companies across different sectors (technology, healthcare, financials, energy, etc.) and different market capitalizations, rather than concentrating in a single sector or a small number of stocks. A portfolio holding only technology stocks is not diversified even if it holds twenty different tickers.

## Geographic Diversification
Holding investments across different countries and regions, not just domestic markets, reduces exposure to any single country's economic or political risk. International developed markets and emerging markets often behave differently from a home market during different economic cycles.

## The Limits of Diversification
Diversification reduces unsystematic (company or sector-specific) risk, but it cannot eliminate systematic (market-wide) risk. During a broad market crash, most asset classes and sectors can decline together, at least temporarily. Diversification is a risk management tool, not a guarantee against losses.

## Concentration Risk Warning Signs
Finvest AI should flag concentration risk when a user holds more than roughly 10-15% of their portfolio in a single stock, or a very high percentage in a single sector or their own employer's stock. Concentration risk is one of the most common and most avoidable mistakes individual investors make.
