# Asset Classes

## What is an Asset Class?
An asset class is a group of investments that share similar characteristics and behave similarly in the market. The four major asset classes are equities (stocks), fixed income (bonds), cash and cash equivalents, and real assets (real estate, commodities). Each asset class carries a different risk and return profile, and mixing them is the foundation of portfolio construction.

## Equities (Stocks)
A stock represents fractional ownership in a company. When you buy a share, you own a small piece of that business and are entitled to a proportional claim on its earnings and assets. Equities historically offer the highest long-term returns among major asset classes, but also the highest volatility. Stocks are further divided into large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap based on company size, and into growth or value styles based on how the market prices their future earnings.

## Fixed Income (Bonds)
A bond is a loan made by an investor to a borrower, typically a corporation or government. In exchange, the borrower pays periodic interest (the coupon) and returns the principal at maturity. Bonds are generally less volatile than stocks and are used to provide income and stability to a portfolio. Bond prices move inversely to interest rates: when rates rise, existing bond prices fall, and vice versa.

## Cash and Cash Equivalents
This category includes savings accounts, money market funds, treasury bills, and certificates of deposit. Cash equivalents offer the lowest risk and lowest expected return of the major asset classes. They are primarily used for capital preservation, short-term goals, and as dry powder to deploy into other assets during market downturns.

## Real Assets
Real estate, commodities such as gold and oil, and infrastructure fall into this category. Real assets often behave differently from stocks and bonds, which makes them useful for diversification. They can also act as a hedge against inflation, since their value tends to rise alongside the cost of goods and services.

## Choosing an Asset Mix
The right mix of asset classes for an investor depends on their time horizon, risk tolerance, and financial goals. A longer time horizon generally allows for a higher allocation to equities, since there is more time to recover from downturns. A shorter time horizon, such as saving for a home purchase in two years, generally calls for a higher allocation to cash and bonds.
