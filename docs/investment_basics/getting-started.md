# Getting Started with Investing

## Why Invest at All?
Money left in a low-interest savings account loses purchasing power over time due to inflation. Investing puts your money to work so it can grow faster than the rate of inflation, helping you build wealth toward goals like retirement, a home purchase, or financial independence.

## Setting a Financial Goal First
Before choosing any investment, define what you are investing for and when you will need the money. A retirement goal 30 years away can tolerate more short-term volatility than a down payment needed in 18 months. Every recommendation in this system should be evaluated against the user's stated goal and time horizon.

## The Power of Starting Early
Because of compounding, the earlier an investor starts, the less principal they need to contribute to reach the same end goal. A 25-year-old investing a modest amount monthly can outperform a 35-year-old investing significantly more per month, purely due to the extra decade of compounding.

## Emergency Fund Before Investing
Financial guidance generally recommends building an emergency fund covering three to six months of essential expenses in a cash or cash-equivalent account before investing significant amounts in the market. This prevents an investor from being forced to sell investments at a loss during a personal financial emergency.

## Common First Mistakes
New investors often make three mistakes: investing money they will need within the next one to two years, trying to time the market instead of investing consistently, and building a portfolio that is not diversified because it is concentrated in a single stock or sector, often their own employer's stock.

## Dollar-Cost Averaging
Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals, regardless of market conditions. This approach reduces the risk of investing a large lump sum right before a downturn and removes the emotional pressure of trying to "pick the right moment" to invest.
