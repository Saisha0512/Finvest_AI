# Financial Glossary

## Asset Allocation
The process of dividing a portfolio among different asset categories, such as stocks, bonds, and cash, based on an investor's goals, risk tolerance, and time horizon.

## Bull Market
A sustained period of rising asset prices, typically defined as a rise of 20% or more from a recent low.

## Bear Market
A sustained period of falling asset prices, typically defined as a decline of 20% or more from a recent high.

## Capital Gain
The profit realized when an investment is sold for more than its purchase price. A capital gain is "unrealized" until the position is actually sold.

## Compounding
The process by which investment returns generate their own additional returns over time, causing growth to accelerate the longer money remains invested.

## Diversification
Spreading investments across different assets to reduce the impact of any single investment's poor performance on the overall portfolio.

## Dividend Yield
The annual dividend payment expressed as a percentage of a stock's current price, calculated as annual dividend per share divided by share price.

## Expense Ratio
The annual fee charged by a fund, expressed as a percentage of assets under management, covering the fund's operating costs.

## Liquidity
How quickly and easily an asset can be converted into cash without significantly affecting its price. Stocks of large companies are highly liquid; real estate is relatively illiquid.

## Market Capitalization
The total market value of a company's outstanding shares, calculated as share price multiplied by total shares outstanding.

## P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)
A valuation metric calculated by dividing a company's share price by its earnings per share, used to assess whether a stock is relatively expensive or cheap compared to its earnings.

## Rebalancing
Adjusting a portfolio's holdings back to its original target allocation after market movements have caused it to drift.

## Risk Tolerance
An investor's psychological willingness to endure fluctuations in the value of their investments in pursuit of higher returns.

## Volatility
The degree of variation in an investment's price over time, commonly measured using standard deviation of returns.

## Yield
The income generated by an investment over a period, typically expressed as a percentage of the investment's value, such as bond yield or dividend yield.
