# Frequently Asked Questions

## How much money do I need to start investing?
There is no strict minimum for most modern brokerage platforms — many allow fractional share purchases, meaning you can start investing with as little as a few dollars. What matters more than the starting amount is starting consistently and giving your investments time to compound.

## Is investing the same as trading?
No. Investing generally refers to buying assets with the intent to hold them for years, aiming to benefit from long-term growth and compounding. Trading refers to more frequent buying and selling, often over days, weeks, or months, aiming to profit from short-term price movements. Trading carries higher risk and requires significantly more time and expertise.

## What's the difference between a Roth and Traditional retirement account?
A Traditional account allows pre-tax contributions, reducing taxable income now, with withdrawals taxed in retirement. A Roth account uses after-tax contributions, so there is no upfront tax deduction, but qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. The better choice generally depends on whether an investor expects to be in a higher or lower tax bracket in retirement compared to now.

## How often should I check my portfolio?
Checking too frequently can lead to emotional, reactive decisions during normal short-term volatility. For long-term investors, reviewing a portfolio quarterly or semi-annually — outside of a scheduled rebalancing check — is generally sufficient unless there has been a major life event.

## Should I sell everything when the market drops?
Selling during a downturn locks in losses and removes the possibility of participating in the eventual recovery. Historically, markets have recovered from every prior downturn, though the timing of recovery is never guaranteed. Long-term investors with a properly diversified, risk-appropriate portfolio are generally better served by staying invested through volatility rather than reacting to short-term declines.

## What is the difference between saving and investing?
Saving means setting aside money in low-risk, highly liquid places like a savings account, primarily for near-term needs and emergencies. Investing means putting money into assets like stocks and bonds with the goal of growing it over a longer time horizon, accepting more risk and less immediate liquidity in exchange for higher expected returns.
